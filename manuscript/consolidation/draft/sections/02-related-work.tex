\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:background}

Edge-of-Stability studies establish that oscillatory training can reduce loss while sharpness remains near or above $2/\eta$~\cite{cohen2021edge,arora2022understanding}. Self-stabilization and Central Flows explain how oscillations interact with curvature and influence slower optimization dynamics~\cite{damian2023self,cohen2025central}. Edge of Stochastic Stability extends the analysis to mini-batch objectives and distinguishes sampling-induced oscillations from instability~\cite{andreyev2025stochastic}. These accounts explain oscillatory stability, while path-length studies bound the distance traveled by optimization trajectories~\cite{gupta2021path}. The complementary question addressed here is how reinforcement and cancellation between individual updates determine the net displacement accumulated during training. We connect gradient magnitudes and temporal alignment to this displacement, distinguishing adjacent opposition from interactions across longer temporal separations and separating fixed-state sampling effects from changes along evolving trajectories.

Bai et al.~\cite{bai2026spikes} explain Adam spikes through decoupling between squared gradients and their running average, but identify the cost of Hessian--vector-product-based monitoring as a scaling limitation. Consecutive-gradient diagnostics already target convergence-stage detection, with known sensitivity to sampling noise~\cite{chee2018diagnostics,pesme2020diagnostics}. Barzilai--Borwein methods provide the classical secant construction underlying our step-direction quantity, which is the reciprocal of BB1 when defined~\cite{barzilai1988two,tan2016bbsgd}. Lee and Lee~\cite{lee2026road} also avoid additional derivative evaluations, using observed loss changes to characterize completed steps. Our contribution concerns a different diagnostic quantity and use: gradient differences and the applied increment recover step-averaged directional curvature for smooth, fixed objectives with full gradients, while separate experiments evaluate gradient alignment and amplification as warnings before the next Adam update. This preserves the distinction between exact completed-step measurement and empirical prediction.

Central Flows explicitly leaves unresolved how loss-landscape dynamics translate into the roles of individual layers~\cite{cohen2025central}. Representation-comparison methods characterize similarities across layers and training states~\cite{raghu2017svcca,kornblith2019similarity}; we examine consecutive representation changes to locate reversal, quantify movement, and identify low-dimensional patterns and shared timing during oscillatory training. Controlling these changes remains a related challenge. Kosson et al.~\cite{kosson2024warmup} identify representation change as a promising measure of update size, but leave effective control open, with their initial intervention results focused on reducing warmup. We test local displacement caps during established oscillatory training. Because loss excursions can accompany improved feature learning~\cite{zhu2024catapults}, our comparisons assess training-loss outcomes alongside fluctuation suppression, asking whether movement control helps while reversals persist.
