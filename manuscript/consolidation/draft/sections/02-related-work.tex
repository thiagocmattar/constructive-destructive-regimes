\section{Stability Analysis of SGD}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Local Curvature and Temporal Gradient Information}
For a fixed positive-definite quadratic, the GD update Jacobian is $I-\eta H$. Its eigencomponents preserve sign for $0<\eta\lambda_i<1$, alternate while contracting for $1<\eta\lambda_i<2$, and grow in magnitude above two. Neural Hessians are strongly anisotropic~\cite{sagun2017hessian,ghorbani2019density}, so the sharpest direction and the realized gradient direction need not coincide. Repeated spectral estimation also requires derivative computations beyond the ordinary training gradient.

Two-point secant methods address a different computational question: they recover scalar curvature information from successive parameters and gradients~\cite{barzilai1988two,tan2016bbsgd}. Consecutive-gradient products also appear in hypergradient adaptation~\cite{baydin2018hypergradient} and constant-step SGD stationarity diagnostics~\cite{chee2018diagnostics}. Pesme et al.~\cite{pesme2020diagnostics} show that interpreting such products requires attention to noise and the statistical difficulty of detection. We credit these established ingredients. Our use combines their directional meaning with all-pair accumulation, a finite-step loss relation, and measured functional changes; a negative product is not treated as a declaration of convergence.

\subsection{Mini-Batch Stochasticity}
Sampling changes both a measured gradient and the future state reached by the optimizer. Stochastic-approximation and diffusion analyses describe these effects through gradient-noise statistics~\cite{mandt2017sgd,hu2019diffusion}. For uniform sampling without replacement within a batch, the covariance has a finite-population correction. This yields exact second-moment identities, but not an exact formula for expected cosine: its random denominator also depends on the sampled gradients. Our experiments separate frozen-parameter sampling from actual SGD continuations and distinguish conditional sampling repetitions from independent initializations.

\subsection{Curvature, Noise, and the Edge of Stability}
The break-even framework connects learning rate, batch size, and the evolution of sharpness and gradient variability~\cite{jastrzebski2020break}. Edge-of-stability observations show short-term nonmonotonic loss alongside longer-term descent~\cite{cohen2021edge}; self-stabilization and central flows explain aspects of these changing-geometry dynamics~\cite{damian2023self,cohen2025central}. Catapult analyses relate loss spikes to leading tangent-kernel directions and feature learning~\cite{zhu2024catapults}. More recent work studies feature structure and NTK eigenspace evolution near this regime~\cite{terjek2025feature,jiang2025ntk}. These results motivate examining both rapid reversals and their accumulation. Our temporal SVD acts on consecutive representation changes; its modes are not Hessian or NTK eigenvectors.

\subsection{Practical Stability and Representation-Sensitive Control}
Under adaptive optimization, curvature must be interpreted through the update transformation. Adaptive edge-of-stability work measures preconditioned geometry~\cite{cohen2022adaptive}. Bai et al.~\cite{bai2026spikes} identify a second-moment tracking mechanism for Adam spikes and examine directional curvature and lower second-moment coefficients. Their threshold concerns preconditioned dynamics with momentum conventions, not an untransformed Hessian or an arbitrary raw-gradient statistic. Our late coefficient switches retain the accumulated optimizer state and are therefore distinct from training with a smaller coefficient from initialization.

Representation similarity is also established: SVCCA and CKA compare activation structure~\cite{raghu2017svcca,kornblith2019similarity}. We instead measure the signed \emph{changes} of matched activation coordinates, preserving time order and movement amplitude. For intervention, Kosson et al.~\cite[Sec.~6 and App.~B.6]{kosson2024warmup} correct relative representation change during warmup; adaptive gradient clipping (AGC) limits unit-wise raw-gradient/weight norm ratios~\cite[Sec.~4]{brock2021normalization}; and FOOF constructs neuron-space descent using an inverse input second-moment factor~\cite[Sec.~4]{benzing2022neurons}. The cap studied here radially truncates an existing Adam increment using absolute, per-unit direct affine RMS and a fixed past-calibrated budget. It uses input geometry without an inverse and acts after the optimizer transformation. This specifies the method's distinction; it does not establish superiority to those representation-aware methods.
