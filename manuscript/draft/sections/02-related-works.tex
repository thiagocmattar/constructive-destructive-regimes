\section{Background and Related Diagnostics}
\label{sec:background}

Training dynamics reflect both the geometry encountered by an update and
its interaction with preceding updates. Curvature-based analyses explain
local amplification; stochastic analyses add sampling variation; temporal
diagnostics describe how successive directions reinforce or oppose one
another. We connect them through curvature information recovered from
training gradients and interpreted through constructive/destructive regimes.

\subsection{Curvature, Oscillation and Stability}

Let $\theta_t\in\mathbb R^p$ denote the parameters after update $t$ and
$g_t$ the gradient evaluated at $\theta_{t-1}$. For a constant learning
rate $\eta>0$, plain SGD follows
\begin{equation}
 \theta_t=\theta_{t-1}-\eta g_t.
 \label{eq:sgd_map_stability}
\end{equation}
In full-batch GD, $g_t=\nabla\mathcal L(\theta_{t-1})$ for the training
objective $\mathcal L$. Writing $H_t=\nabla^2\mathcal L(\theta_{t-1})$,
the deterministic update has Jacobian
\begin{equation}
 J_t=I-\eta H_t.
 \label{eq:jacobian_sgd}
\end{equation}
This matrix describes the response to a small parameter perturbation.
For a fixed positive-definite quadratic Hessian, each eigencomponent is
multiplied by $1-\eta\lambda_i$, and linear stability requires
\begin{equation}
 \max_i|1-\eta\lambda_i|<1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \eta\lambda_{\max}(H)<2.
 \label{eq:quad_stability_condition}
\end{equation}
The component contracts without changing sign when $0<\eta\lambda_i<1$,
alternates while contracting when $1<\eta\lambda_i<2$, and alternates
with increasing magnitude when $\eta\lambda_i>2$~\cite{polyak1964methods,
bertsekas1997nonlinear}. Thus oscillation and amplification are distinct
features even in the simplest local model.

Deep-network trajectories repeatedly enter oscillatory regions. Cohen
et al.~\cite{cohen2021edge} show that full-batch GD often approaches
$\lambda_{\max}\simeq2/\eta$ while reducing loss over longer timescales.
Analyses of this Edge-of-Stability behavior explain how nonlinear dynamics
sustain progress near the quadratic boundary~\cite{arora2022understanding,
damian2023self}. The trajectory measurements of Xing et al.~\cite{xing2018walk}
likewise describe steps crossing between sides of a valley. These results
motivate examining the gradients produced by successive crossings:
their alignment reveals reversal, and their magnitudes reveal whether
that reversal is shrinking or growing.

\subsection{Sampling Noise and Local Geometry}

Mini-batch sampling changes both the estimated direction and the trajectory
followed through the objective~\cite{mandt2017sgd, hu2019diffusion,
jastrzebski2018three}. Under fresh, conditionally unbiased sampling, write
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
  g_t&=\nabla\mathcal L(\theta_{t-1})+\xi_t,\\
  \mathbb E[\xi_t\mid\theta_{t-1}]&=0,\\
  \operatorname{Cov}(\xi_t\mid\theta_{t-1})
   &=\frac{N-b}{b(N-1)}\Sigma(\theta_{t-1}),
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:sgd_noise_decomposition}
\end{equation}
where the last equality assumes a uniformly sampled size-$b$ subset of
$N>1$ examples without replacement. At a fixed state, let
$h_i=\nabla\ell_i(\theta)$ and
$\mu=N^{-1}\sum_i h_i=\nabla\mathcal L(\theta)$. The per-example covariance is
\begin{equation}
 \Sigma(\theta)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N
                 (h_i-\mu)(h_i-\mu)^\top.
 \label{eq:grad_cov_def}
\end{equation}
Its trace measures the total squared dispersion around the full gradient.
The finite-population factor in Eq.~\eqref{eq:sgd_noise_decomposition}
shows how larger batches reduce this dispersion.

Curvature and stochasticity also interact. The break-even analysis of
Jastrzebski et al.~\cite{jastrzebski2020break} links the early learning rate
and batch size to the curvature and gradient variability encountered later.
In its local one-dimensional quadratic model, variation in sampled curvature
adds to the mean-square amplification produced by the mean curvature.
This gives a reason to examine the regime along the evolving path as well
as the sampling variance at a fixed point. Section~\ref{subsec:sampling-noise}
makes that distinction explicit for adjacent-gradient alignment.

\subsection{Temporal Gradient Diagnostics}

Successive gradient products have a substantial history in optimization
diagnostics. Chee and Toulis~\cite{chee2018diagnostics} use them to detect
the transition toward a stationary phase of constant-step SGD.
Pesme et al.~\cite{pesme2020diagnostics} analyze the classical
gradient-product test and show how its small signal relative to variation
can impair phase detection. Baydin et al.~\cite{baydin2018hypergradient}
derive online learning-rate adaptation from hypergradients; for ordinary
gradient descent, the resulting update uses the inner product of successive
gradients. Reusing gradient differences to approximate curvature also
underlies the two-point step sizes of Barzilai and
Borwein~\cite{barzilai1988two}.

We use this curvature information to explain the regime's anatomy:
adjacent alignment identifies reversal, norm growth determines its strength,
and cumulative displacement measures its accumulated effect. This joint
interpretation distinguishes productive destructive descent from amplified
loss excursions and extends to raw-gradient response under Adam.

\subsection{Loss Spikes in Practical Training}
\label{subsec:practical-stability}

Large-model training reports show why early diagnostics matter. PaLM
describes approximately 20 loss spikes during 540B-model training and
recovers by restarting from an earlier checkpoint and skipping
batches~\cite{chowdhery2022palm}. OPT reports growing activation norms
and loss divergence, with recovery through checkpoint restarts and
learning-rate reduction; tighter gradient clipping also improves early
stability~\cite{zhang2022opt}. These interventions incur training cost
and make the timing of a warning practically consequential.

Modern recipes therefore monitor gradient norms alongside loss.
OLMo~2 uses rolling loss and gradient-norm spike scores in its stability
analysis~\cite{olmo2025furious}, while Llama~3 emphasizes numerical
precision, warmup and batch-size scheduling in a largely stable
pretraining recipe~\cite{grattafiori2024llama3}. Adjacent-gradient cosine
adds a directional interpretation to such magnitude measurements:
it identifies whether a growing gradient reinforces or opposes the
preceding one. The following section develops this combination into
diagnostics of constructive and destructive training.
