\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Constructive and destructive gradient interactions describe how parameter
changes reinforce or cancel one another at a specified temporal scale.
The displacement decomposition and paired factors make that description
quantitative: adjacent cancellation and coherent accumulation of residual
motion can occur together. The controlled GD results demonstrate this
coexistence, with loss measurements establishing progress separately.

The curvature and sampling relations explain the scope of the local
diagnostic. Consecutive gradients recover a classical directional secant
under plain GD; sampling and optimizer transformations change its
interpretation. The Adam results make that distinction concrete: every
measured rise-phase step in the recovered-event sample points locally
downhill, although the finite trajectory rises in loss. Matched interventions
and Pythia training further show that making raw-gradient alignment more
positive, clipping the optimizer input or suppressing a particular excursion
does not consistently improve the
chosen loss outcome.

This framework turns a qualitative observation of oscillatory training into
an account of where cancellation occurs, which vectors participate, and how
the measured motion relates to the objective. Its immediate use is to
formulate and evaluate explanations of training behavior. Whether an
intervention should reduce cancellation, preserve it while controlling step
magnitude, or modify optimizer memory is a separate empirical question.
The present evidence establishes the diagnostic distinctions needed to ask
that question precisely; it does not select a universally beneficial action.
