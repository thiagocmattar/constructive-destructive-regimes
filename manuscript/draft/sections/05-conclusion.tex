\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The displacement formulation distinguishes the magnitude of individual updates,
their local directional interaction and their cumulative accumulation. For
plain SGD, the exact decomposition retains all temporal lags, while consecutive
cosine provides a tractable local observation. The curvature and sampling
relations clarify how that observation can change without determining either
cumulative displacement or loss improvement. The full-batch experiments show negative consecutive interaction alongside positive total interaction across all pairs. Pairing adjacent updates identifies substantial immediate cancellation, followed by model-dependent accumulation of the residual motion. Neither factor guarantees a local loss decrease. The mini-batch continuations further show that observed alignment changes with both sampling and the evolving trajectory; weaker reversal alone does not order the attained loss.

Under Adam, loss-defined excursions connect amplification and recovery
with changes in the raw loss slope. Ordinary-trajectory controls show
that reversal and a projected response above two also occur without a
pronounced short-horizon loss rise. Norm amplification accounts for
substantial severity variation, while the incremental contribution of
alignment varies and does not transfer consistently across rates.
All recorded rise-phase steps have a locally descending first-order
component, distinguishing raw-gradient reversal from an uphill update.

The analytical Adam extension relates projected gradient response to a
one-HVP preconditioned-curvature proxy through first-moment discrepancy
and finite-step curvature variation. Adjacent gradient sums drive the
memory residual, connecting this interpretation to GD pairing. These
identities specify a calibration question; agreement on the neural
trajectories has not yet been established. Independent initializations,
matched optimizer-state experiments and same-objective stochastic probes
are the next tests. Claims of practical monitoring advantage additionally
require measured overhead and fidelity against the one-HVP baseline.
