\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The displacement formulation distinguishes the magnitude of individual updates,
their local directional interaction and their cumulative accumulation. For
plain SGD, the exact decomposition retains all temporal lags, while consecutive
cosine provides a tractable local observation. The curvature and sampling
relations clarify how that observation can change without determining either
cumulative displacement or loss improvement. The full-batch and mini-batch
experiments illustrate these distinctions: gradient reversal can coexist with
objective progress, and weaker reversal need not improve displacement retention
or the attained loss.

In Adam, raw-gradient dynamics provide information about impending loss
excursions beyond recent loss history, even though the optimizer transforms
those gradients before updating the parameters. The controlled full-batch
comparisons support this interpretation through forward-time prediction
and the timing of gradient/momentum changes around spikes. Local reversal
can coexist with coherent adaptive directions and continued objective
progress. Frequent threshold false alarms distinguish the information in
the continuous diagnostic from a universal spike trigger.

The practical contribution is a framework for interpreting these measurements
together and using them to formulate controlled interventions. Developing a
scheduler requires a further step: demonstrating that acting on the diagnostics
improves loss beyond decisions based on recent loss and known training settings,
at matched budgets and across independent runs. That intervention evaluation
remains future work.
