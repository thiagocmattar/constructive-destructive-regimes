\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The displacement formulation distinguishes the magnitude of individual updates,
their local directional interaction and their cumulative accumulation. For
plain SGD, the exact decomposition retains all temporal lags, while consecutive
cosine provides a tractable local observation. The curvature and sampling
relations clarify how that observation can change without determining either
cumulative displacement or loss improvement. The full-batch and mini-batch
experiments illustrate these distinctions: gradient reversal can coexist with
objective progress, and weaker reversal need not improve displacement retention
or the attained loss.

Under Adam, the same measurements acquire different meanings at successive
stages of the optimizer. Raw gradients describe the sampled loss response;
first moments expose temporal averaging; adaptive directions incorporate
coordinate scaling. Parameter displacement additionally depends on the applied
learning rates and weight decay. The language-model comparisons show why
positive momentum or adaptive alignment cannot serve as a universal performance
criterion, and why restoring positive raw alignment through decay need not
improve the final validation loss.

The practical contribution is a framework for interpreting these measurements
together and using them to formulate controlled interventions. Developing a
scheduler requires a further step: demonstrating that the diagnostics improve
rate decisions beyond recent loss and known training settings, at matched
budgets and across independent runs. That evaluation remains future work.
