\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Constructive and destructive regimes provide an interpretable view of
neural-network training through gradients already computed by the optimizer.
Adjacent cosine identifies the regime, gradient norms measure its
amplification, and normalized displacement captures cumulative motion.
The local loss relation explains the central distinction: a destructive
regime can sustain descent, while sufficiently strong opposing gradients
can produce loss-increasing steps. Controlled GD and SGD experiments show
how these regimes evolve with learning rate and mini-batch sampling.

The Adam results establish the relevance of the same raw-gradient view
along adaptive trajectories. Diagnostic changes precede the largest
loss peaks, and the event analysis connects spike magnitude to a
predominantly destructive initial regime followed by gradient amplification.
The micro-plots supply temporal evidence, while the distributions and
regressions show how the pattern repeats across models and rates.
Together, they identify observable early signs and a consistent anatomy
of local loss instability.

The resulting framework combines inexpensive measurement with a concrete
interpretation of the trajectory. It motivates interventions timed to
gradient amplification within a destructive regime, including learning-rate
adjustment, gradient clipping and data mixing. Prospective warning rules
and matched intervention tests are the next step toward using these
diagnostics to reduce spikes while preserving training progress.
