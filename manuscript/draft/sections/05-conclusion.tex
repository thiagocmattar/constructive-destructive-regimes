\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Consecutive training gradients make directional curvature observable
without Hessian computation. For smooth full-batch GD, the estimate
equals curvature averaged along the step and approximates its starting-point
value; one retained gradient and linear-time reductions support online
monitoring. Adjacent cosine identifies constructive and destructive regimes,
and the norm ratio expresses the amplification entering the estimate.
The local loss relation explains the distinction: destructive training
can sustain descent, while sufficiently amplified reversal produces
loss-increasing steps. Controlled GD and SGD experiments characterize
these regimes and their cumulative displacement.

Under Adam, the same measurements reveal projected curvature response
along adaptive trajectories. Diagnostic changes precede the largest
loss peaks, and the event analysis connects spike magnitude to a
predominantly destructive initial regime followed by gradient amplification.
The micro-plots supply temporal evidence, while the distributions and
regressions show how the pattern repeats across models and rates.
Together, they identify observable early signs and a consistent anatomy
of local loss instability.

This connection between curvature, regime and amplification provides an
interpretable basis for stabilization heuristics. Learning-rate adjustment,
clipping and momentum act on the step or gradient dynamics exposed by
the diagnostics. Prospective warning rules and matched intervention tests
can turn this online information into decisions that attenuate spikes
while preserving training progress.
