\section{Derivation of the Alignment-Based Loss Change}
\label{appendix:loss-curvature}

Consider the SGD update
$\theta_{t-1}=\theta_{t-2}-\eta g_{t-1}$.
The change in the full objective along this step can be
expressed exactly as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\mathcal{L}(\theta_{t-1})-\mathcal{L}(\theta_{t-2})\\
&\quad=-\eta\int_0^1
g_{t-1}^\top
\nabla\mathcal{L}(\theta_{t-2}-s\eta g_{t-1})\,ds.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:appendix-loss-integral}
\end{equation}
Approximating the integral by the average of the full gradients
at the two endpoints gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\mathcal{L}(\theta_{t-1})-\mathcal{L}(\theta_{t-2})\\
&\quad\approx-\frac{\eta}{2}g_{t-1}^\top
\left(
\nabla\mathcal{L}(\theta_{t-2})
+\nabla\mathcal{L}(\theta_{t-1})
\right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:appendix-loss-average}
\end{equation}
This approximation is exact for quadratic objectives.
For nonlinear objectives, the approximation error is of
third order in the step length when the Hessian is locally
Lipschitz continuous.

Using the stochastic gradient decomposition
$g_t=\nabla\mathcal{L}(\theta_{t-1})+\xi_t$
at both endpoints, we obtain
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\mathcal{L}(\theta_{t-1})-\mathcal{L}(\theta_{t-2})\\
&\quad\approx-\frac{\eta}{2}g_{t-1}^\top
(g_{t-1}+g_t-\xi_{t-1}-\xi_t)\\
&\quad=-\frac{\eta}{2}
\left(\|g_{t-1}\|^2+g_{t-1}^\top g_t\right)\\
&\qquad+\frac{\eta}{2}
g_{t-1}^\top(\xi_{t-1}+\xi_t).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:appendix-loss-gradient}
\end{equation}
Substituting
$g_{t-1}^\top g_t
=\|g_{t-1}\|\|g_t\|\rho_t$
and factoring the preceding squared gradient norm yields
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\mathcal{L}(\theta_{t-1})-\mathcal{L}(\theta_{t-2})\\
&\quad\approx
-\frac{\eta}{2}\|g_{t-1}\|^2
\left(1+\rho_t\frac{\|g_t\|}{\|g_{t-1}\|}\right)\\
&\qquad+\frac{\eta}{2}
g_{t-1}^\top(\xi_{t-1}+\xi_t),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
which recovers Eq.~\eqref{eq:loss-curvature}.

To connect this expression with directional curvature,
recall from Eq.~\eqref{eq:rho-normalized} that
\begin{equation}
  \rho_t\frac{\|g_t\|}{\|g_{t-1}\|}
  \approx 1-\eta\kappa_t+\varepsilon_t.
\end{equation}
Substituting this relation and using the definition
of $\varepsilon_t$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:epsilon} gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\mathcal{L}(\theta_{t-1})-\mathcal{L}(\theta_{t-2})\\
&\quad\approx-\eta\|g_{t-1}\|^2
\left(1-\frac{\eta\kappa_t}{2}\right)
+\eta\xi_{t-1}^\top g_{t-1}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:appendix-loss-curvature}
\end{equation}
Thus, the curvature-based and alignment-based expressions
describe the same local loss change to second order.
Positive directional curvature counteracts the first-order
loss decrease, while its effect is already incorporated
into the weighted alignment between consecutive gradients.
The noise contributions account for the difference between
the observed mini-batch gradients and the full gradients.





\section{Sample-Level Decomposition for the Image-Classification Results}
\label{appendix:sample-level}

The image-classification results also report a sample-level decomposition.
For those results and this appendix, index mini-batches by $t=0,\ldots,T-1$,
write their size as $m$, and let $g_i^{(t)}$ be the gradient of sample $i$
evaluated at $\theta_t$. The batch mean is
\[
  G_{B_t}:=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^m g_i^{(t)}.
\]
Thus, $G_{B_t}$ in this indexing is $g_{t+1}$ in
Section~\ref{sec:displacement}. For constant-step plain SGD,
\begin{equation}
  \theta_T=\theta_0-\eta\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}G_{B_t}.
  \label{eq:final_theta_expression}
\end{equation}
Taking the squared norm gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\|\theta_T\|^2={}&\|\theta_0\|^2
 -2\eta\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\theta_0^\top G_{B_t}\\
 &+\eta^2\Bigl\|\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}G_{B_t}\Bigr\|^2.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:norm_split_terms}
\end{equation}
Define the initialization--trajectory term
$C:=-2\eta\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\theta_0^\top G_{B_t}$ and the sample-level sums
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
S_1&:=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\sum_{i=1}^m\|g_i^{(t)}\|^2,\\
S_2&:=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\sum_{i=1}^m
       \sum_{\substack{j=1\\j\ne i}}^m g_i^{(t)\top}g_j^{(t)},\\
S_3&:=2\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\sum_{u=0}^{t-1}
       \sum_{i,j=1}^m g_i^{(t)\top}g_j^{(u)}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:s_def}
\end{equation}
The sums collect squared per-sample gradient norms, within-batch interactions,
and all cross-step interactions, respectively. Their expansion gives the exact
identity
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\|\theta_T\|^2={}&\|\theta_0\|^2+C\\
 &+\frac{\eta^2}{m^2}(S_1+S_2+S_3).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:thetaT_full}
\end{equation}
In particular, $(S_1+S_2)/m^2=\sum_t\|G_{B_t}\|^2$ is the sum of squared
batch-gradient norms, while $S_3/m^2$ is the complete temporal interaction. The
latter cannot generally be recovered from consecutive cosine alone. These
identities specify the quantities displayed in Fig.~\ref{fig:traj_decomp}; they do not
identify their signs with loss reduction or a stability guarantee.
